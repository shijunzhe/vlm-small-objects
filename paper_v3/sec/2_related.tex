\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{High-resolution VLMs.} The accuracy of VLMs drops with object size and with distractors~\cite{zhang2026perceptual,zhang2025mllmsknow}, and they lose small targets in large or cluttered inputs~\cite{wu2025visualhaystacks,sharma2024losing,zhang2025hrscene,zhang2025mmerealworld}. Dynamic tiling and any-resolution encoders~\cite{chen2024internvl15,xu2024llavauhd,bai2025qwen25vl} change the model to use more resolution. We leave the model fixed and derive what the interface allows in one call, a ceiling on $S$, and what seeing a whole image must cost.

\paragraph{Splitting and zooming at inference time.} Slicing Aided Hyper Inference (SAHI) cuts fixed-size slices at native resolution with a fixed overlap ratio for a detector~\cite{akyon2022sahi}; its slices never zoom out, but their $S$ is set by the object's pixel size rather than by what the model needs. DC$^2$ splits recursively into quadrants and merges text descriptions~\cite{wang2025dc2}, V$^\ast$ searches quadrants guided by a heatmap~\cite{wu2024vstar}, and ZoomEye runs a tree search over sub-patches ranked by the model's confidence~\cite{shen2024zoomeye}; ViCrop picks one crop from the model's attention or gradients~\cite{zhang2025mllmsknow}. These methods let heuristics or the model decide where to look. We add a protection condition that a caller can check before any call, the minimum cost of protection, and a construction within an explicit factor of it; for example, a $2 \times 2$ split without overlap leaves 9 to 19\% of our targets cut by a boundary (\supp{G}). Agents that zoom while reasoning~\cite{openai2025thinking,zheng2026deepeyes,lai2026minio3,zhang2025chainoffocus,su2025thinkingwithimages} choose their own views; \cref{prop:frontier} bounds what such a strategy must spend when recognition is graded, and we test four such agents (\cref{sec:practice}). \citet{liu2026hide} argue that zooming helps mainly by removing background rather than by enlarging the target; $S$ and $L$ separate the two effects, and which one dominates depends on the model (\cref{sec:agents}).

\paragraph{Many similar objects.} Errors of VLMs grow with the number of objects and with how many features they share, which \citet{campbell2024binding} attribute to the binding problem. Autoregressive detectors tend to stop before they have listed every object, and delaying the stop yields noisy and duplicated predictions~\cite{chen2022pix2seq}. We measure the combined effect of such limits at the level of a call, and give the remedy that a caller controls. Our companion study~\cite{companion2026readout} shows that how a call is asked to report (its request, coordinate format and output structure) changes the returned set; we hold the request and format fixed and ask how the scale and content of a call decide what it can find, and when splitting it is safe.

\paragraph{Counting and visual prompts.} Class-agnostic counters localize instances from exemplars~\cite{amininaieni2024countgd,pelhan2026geco2}, and VLM counting has been improved by dividing the image~\cite{qharabagh2026lvlmcount}. Benchmarks include FSC-147~\cite{ranjan2021fsc147}, CountBench~\cite{paiss2023countbench}, PixMo-Count~\cite{deitke2025molmo} and CountQA~\cite{tamarapalli2025countqa}, where accuracy falls as counts rise. Overlays such as marks, grids, labelled dots and scan lines are reported to help grounding and counting~\cite{yang2023setofmark,lei2025scaffold,izadi2025viser}; by \cref{prop:dpi}, an overlay on the sent image adds no Shannon information about the targets.

\paragraph{Symbol spotting in drawings.} Symbol spotting and floor-plan parsing have dedicated datasets and methods~\cite{liu2017raster,kalervo2019cubicasa,fan2021floorplancad,luo2025archcad}, surveyed by \citet{morenogarcia2019trends}; FloorPlanCAD and ArchCAD-400K train dedicated models for panoptic symbol spotting on large annotated sets. Recent benchmarks show that VLMs struggle to recognize and count symbols on drawings~\cite{shteriyanov2025blueprintsymvl,kondratenko2026aecv,jung2026drawingvqa}, and normalized cross-correlation template matching~\cite{lewis1995fast,brunelli2009template} is a strong, training-free baseline when a clean exemplar is available. We study VLM calls with one example and no training.

\paragraph{Diagnosing VLM vision.} VLMs fail simple geometric tasks~\cite{rahmanzadehgervi2024blind}; attention analyses show how image information reaches text tokens~\cite{kaduri2025whats} and how localization is computed~\cite{schaumloeffel2026mechanisms}. These works study a model's inside; we study the scale, content and cost of a call. Usable information~\cite{xu2020usable} explains why enlarging can help without adding Shannon information.
