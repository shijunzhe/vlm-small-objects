\section{Experimental protocol}
\label{sec:protocol}

\paragraph{Task and answer.} Each call gives the model an image (or a view) and one reference image of the target and asks for a JSON (JavaScript Object Notation) object with a count and the list of centres of every instance, in the model's published convention (pixels, or 0 to 1000). When an image is split, points outside a view's core are discarded and the rest mapped back to the image. Prompts are in \supp{C}.

\paragraph{Data.} We use \CscaleImages{} images of four kinds: 50 architectural drawings, 40 of which form LegendSpot (1{,}028 instances; 6 sheets used only to write the prompt, and 34 test drawings: 14 full sheets and 20 small drawings) and 10 whose reference symbols come from a symbol library; 429 floor plans from FloorPlanCAD~\cite{fan2021floorplancad} (369 single-block items and 60 mosaics); 150 natural images from FSC-147~\cite{ranjan2021fsc147}; and \CscaleCtrl{} controlled images, 12 of them for the controlled test and the rest for recognition, load and newest-generation measurements. Details in \supp{C}; earlier versions of this paper use internal names for the data sets, listed in \supp{M}.

\paragraph{Models and settings.} We use \CscaleModels{} models from five families (GPT, Claude, Gemini, Gemma, Qwen), each read in its published coordinate convention. Claude Sonnet 5.5 and Opus 5.5 run with the API's default adaptive thinking. The Qwen3-VL agent uses the instruct model without reasoning. Our \emph{standard input} is input that we resize ourselves so that the vendor need not resize it again: whole-image calls with a long side of at most 1{,}600 px (in the controlled test, at most 2{,}048 px and within each vendor's budget), and views within the budgets of \cref{tab:thumb}; the OpenAI models keep all these inputs at their sent size. Reasoning is off where the interface allows it and otherwise at its lowest setting (GPT-6.1 at its lowest effort, Gemini 3.8 at the minimal thinking budget, Gemini 4 at the lowest thinking level). Two kinds of input lie outside the standard input: native-resolution drawings in a high-resolution mode, and 2{,}048 px images that GPT-5.4 resizes itself. Other settings are in \supp{C}.

\paragraph{Metrics and statistics.} \emph{Hit recall} is the fraction of targets with a returned point within $\tau$; \emph{matched recall} is $\mathrm{TP}/n$, with $\mathrm{TP}$ (true positives) the size of a maximum one-to-one matching within $\tau$; precision is $\mathrm{TP}$ over the returned points, and F1 is their harmonic mean. Hit recall is at least matched recall, with equality when targets are more than $2\tau$ apart. Theory tests use hit recall, configuration comparisons matched recall, precision and F1. With the image weight $a(X) = 1/n(X)$ of \cref{sec:safe}, the theorems apply to mean recall per image. We use paired bootstrap with 4{,}000 resamples over images (source drawings for mosaics), and Holm correction for the agent comparisons. The \emph{44 px recipe} rescales an image so that the target spans 44 px and cuts 1{,}024 px tiles; its \emph{fixed-grid counterpart} instead sets each tile's field of view by \cref{prop:geometry}(c) so that the target spans 1.5 (Gemma 4) or 2.5 (Gemini) tokens. Recall, precision and F1 are fractions; differences are absolute unless marked as relative, and relative gains are given with both ends.
